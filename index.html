Discover. Connect. Create the next step.

This is not a job-hunting worksheet. It is a field guide for five NUS people entering one room with investors, allocators, banks, family offices, venture firms and ecosystem leaders. Use it to turn conversations into insight, relationships and possible NUS–industry opportunities.

Before you walk in

Our mindset: We are not here to collect business cards. We are here to discover where finance is going, understand what talent the market needs, meet people worth knowing, and identify ways NUS can create value for the ecosystem.

Our shared mission

  1. Each student has at least 3 meaningful conversations.
  2. Each student captures one insight about the industry and one insight about talent.
  3. Susan & Danny identify 5 people worth developing as relationships.
  4. As a team, identify 2 concrete follow-up possibilities: recruitment, mentoring, webinar, guest session, project, internship or Gamma Prime–NUS collaboration.
  5. Before leaving, we reconvene and compare discoveries rather than simply comparing contact lists.

The conversation ladder

Don't jump straight to “Are you hiring?” Let the conversation earn the next question.

1 · NOTICEStart from something real
“I was interested in your point about [topic]. What do you think is changing fastest?”
2 · EXPLOREUnderstand their world
“How is that changing the work your team actually does?”
3 · ELICITDiscover the talent need
“What capabilities are becoming more valuable as that happens?”
4 · CONNECTRelate it to yourself/NUS
“That’s interesting — we have MSc Finance students graduating in six weeks who are developing those skills.”
5 · OPENCreate a next step
“Would it be useful to explore a student conversation, mentoring session or recruiting connection with NUS?”

Student introduction

Keep it to about 20 seconds. Adapt the bracketed section.

“Hi, I’m [Name], an MSc Finance student at NUS, graduating in about six weeks. I’m particularly interested in [investment / asset management / private markets / fintech / digital assets / banking]. I’m here because I’d love to understand where the industry is heading and what capabilities people entering the field should be building now.”

Then ask:

“What are you seeing from your side that someone graduating today should really understand?”

For Susan & Danny: don't sell NUS too early

First understand the person's priorities. Then connect NUS to something they have told you.

“We work with the NUS MSc Finance programme. We’re particularly interested in understanding what firms are looking for in emerging finance talent. What are you finding hardest to hire for right now?”

If there is a fit: “That’s exactly the sort of capability we’d like our students to understand. Would you be open to exploring a small engagement with NUS — perhaps mentoring, a practitioner webinar, a guest session or recruitment conversation?”

Conversation prompts

Use one, then follow the answer. The goal is a conversation, not an interview.

Industry
“What change in the investment landscape are people underestimating?”
Work
“How is that change affecting what your team actually spends time doing?”
Talent
“What do you wish young finance professionals understood before joining your field?”
Skills
“Which capability separates someone who can contribute quickly from someone who needs a lot of training?”
Career
“If you were graduating from an MSc Finance programme today, what would you prioritise over the next six months?”
Network
“Who else here would you suggest I speak with if I’m interested in this area?”

If the conversation becomes interesting… go deeper

WHY“What led you to that view?”
EXAMPLE“Could you give me an example from your work?”
IMPLICATION“What does that mean for someone entering the industry?”
ADVICE“What would you do if you were in my position?”

Our check-in: compare discoveries, not contacts

The final 10-minute huddle

Before we leave, each person gets 90 seconds.

  1. “The most useful thing I learned was…”
  2. “The most interesting person I met was… because…”
  3. “The opportunity I see is…”
  4. “The next action I recommend is…”

Our final question: “What can NUS do next that would make this relationship valuable to them?”

After the summit: 48-hour rule

  • Send a short, specific LinkedIn/email follow-up.
  • Refer to one thing they actually said — not “great meeting you at the event”.
  • If there is a genuine NUS opportunity, propose one small next step rather than a large partnership.
  • Put every promising relationship into a simple follow-up list with an owner and date.
